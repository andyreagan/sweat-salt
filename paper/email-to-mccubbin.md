Dr McCubbin,

I read your Performance Nutrition review and the 2023 EJSS modelling paper behind it, and I have a
question about one node of Figure 3 before I decide whether it is worth writing up.

Rearranging the Kurtz–Nguyen equation with post-exercise plasma sodium set equal to pre-exercise gives
the replacement fraction at which plasma sodium stops rising:

    f* = 1 − 1.03 ([Na]sweat + [K]sweat) / ([Na]plasma + 23.8)

which is the closed form of the parameter independence you reported in 2023. For 50 mmol/L that is 66%,
consistent with your 70% heuristic; for 80 mmol/L it is 47%.

The case I keep coming back to: a 70 kg runner with whole-body sweat sodium of 80 mmol/L, sweating
1.4 L/h and drinking 1.0 L/h in a hot marathon. At 3.5 h that is a 2.0% deficit and a projected plasma
sodium of 135.3; at 4 h, 2.3% and 134.6. Your Table 2 frames the >4 h criterion as "mostly," and your
three practitioner questions have no duration term, but Figure 3 puts duration first and makes the
<4 h branch terminal, so this athlete is routed to "season to taste." Run past that gate, the figure's
own >60 mmol/L, 70–90% replacement cell gives 40–70% of sodium losses, and Equation 2 gives 48%. So the
terminal cells are right and only the gate disagrees with them.

Two questions:

1. Is the sub-4 h, high-sweat-sodium, high-replacement case one you considered and set aside as too
   rare to complicate the figure, or is the duration gate meant as a hard criterion?
2. Do you have a sense, from your testing data, of how many athletes sit above ~60 mmol/L whole-body
   and replace more than 60% of losses in 2.5–4 h events? I would like to attach a number to how often
   the gate misroutes before claiming it matters.

I have a short note drafted proposing that the duration node be moved from the top of the tree to a
magnitude check at the bottom, and a small calculator that runs the procedure. I would rather send it
to you first than to the journal. Happy to share either.
