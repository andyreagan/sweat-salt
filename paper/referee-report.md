## Blockers

1. **Does not cite McCubbin 2023 (verified).** The review's ref. 111 is McCubbin AJ, *Eur J Sport Sci* 2023;23(6):992–1000,
   "Modelling sodium requirements of athletes across a variety of exercise scenarios – identifying when to test and
   target, or season to taste." Its abstract states the models "are not influenced by sweat rate, exercise duration,
   body mass, and only to a minor extent by baseline plasma sodium and sweat potassium." The letter presents
   parameter-independence as its finding. It is his. Cite it, and re-scope the contribution to the closed form for f*
   and the duration-gate critique.
2. **"Upper quartile" is wrong.** The referee reports Barnes 2019 whole-body sweat Na as 36.1 ± 10.9 mmol/L,
   range 11.2–86.5 (not verifiable from the abstract; check the paper). If so, the upper quartile is ~44 mmol/L,
   f* ≈ 70%, i.e. exactly McCubbin's heuristic. f* ≤ 50% needs ~76 mmol/L, several SD out. The tex has been changed
   to say "athletes above ~60 mmol/L, the top bin of the review's own Figure 3", which is defensible from Table 1.
3. **Fig. 3 already gets the counterexample right (verified).** Past the duration gate, an 80 mmol/L sweater at 71%
   replacement lands in the ">60 mmol/L, 70–90% replacement" cell: "~40–70% of losses." Eq. 2 gives 48%. The only
   thing that fails is the >4 h gate being first and terminal. The paper should say so and narrow to that claim.

## Major

4. **McCubbin's Table 2 already calls duration a proxy (verified).** Column "Situations for use": "When desired fluid
   replacement is > 70% of losses (**mostly** duration > 4 h and sweat rate < 1800 ml·h⁻¹)." The three practitioner
   questions contain no duration criterion. The strongest version of the letter: the text treats duration as a
   gloss, Fig. 3 promotes it to a hard, terminal gate; text and figure disagree.
5. **80 mmol/L "within the range reported by Barnes"** is true and misleading; it is near the maximum of 1303 athletes,
   and Barnes' whole-body values are regression-predicted from patches. Label it as a bounding case or use ~60.
6. **Ref. 5 (Costill & Saltin 1974) does not support 1.0–1.5 L/h absorption or trainability.** It is a gastric-emptying
   study. Replaced in the tex with Leiper 2015 (*Nutr Rev* 73(S2):57–72) for the absorption range and Jeukendrup 2017
   (*Sports Med* 47(S1):101–110) for trainability. Read both before submitting.
7. **123 mg per 100 mL is above, not "at", the palatability ceiling.** The review gives ~100 mg/100 mL in the EAH and
   pre-exercise sections and ~120 in the sports-drink section. Tex now says it exceeds the ~100–120 range and that the
   dose must come from capsules or food, which is itself a finding.
8. **f is drink ÷ sweat; McCubbin's 70% is total gain ÷ total loss** (includes metabolic water and respiratory loss).
   State the full assumption list under Eq. 1: no urine, no sodium or potassium intake, no metabolic water, no
   respiratory loss, drink absorbed 1:1.
9. **"Why publish modelling with no new data?"** Pre-empt it with a prevalence number: given Barnes' distribution and
   plausible sweat-rate/duration distributions, what fraction of athletes does the >4 h gate route to "season to
   taste" when Eq. 2 says they need sodium? If ~0.1%, this is Correspondence. If a few percent, it is a paper.
   Not yet computed.
